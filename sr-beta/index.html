//  What it does

Multitrack recording for live shows, built for FOH. Record every channel from any console, play it back for a virtual soundcheck, and leave the gig with a finished stereo mix and stems, mixed and mastered inside the app.

  • Any console, any audio interface: Dante, MADI, USB
  • Virtual soundcheck: every track back out its own output
  • The finished mix built in: EQ, compression, tape, clipper and limiter on the buses and master
  • Find Songs, then export the stereo mix and stems per song, loudness measured
  • Record at 48 kHz from a 96 kHz system, converted as it records
  • Show-safe: disk time and FAT32 warnings, crash recovery, dropouts marked, Lock
  • Timecode in and out: stamped takes, record on timecode, follow in playback

//  New in 0.2.15

Since 0.2.14. The full list comes with the download.

Four consoles, by their own names.

The header names the console MIDI Bridge is on (“v0.2.15 · Wing”, “· DiGiCo”, “· SQ”). New Session from the console names a desk’s mixes as the desk does — a Wing bus is “Bus 1”, the SQ’s main is “LR” — and reads the DiGiCo SD’s channels, auxes, groups and matrices. A stereo channel on the SD or the Wing becomes one stereo track.

Scene markers that match the desk.

On the Wing a marker carries the scene’s name (“Scene 4: UP1”); on the SD, the snapshot’s number as its screen shows it (“Snapshot 1.93: Verse”), and Recall in Playback recalls that snapshot.

AUTO: record when timecode runs.

A button in the transport row (and Transport → Record When Timecode Runs) arms recording on timecode for the session: off for a line check, on for the show.

Meters for every row.

A stereo track shows two meters, L and R. The Sub-Mixer and Sync rows — the L/R Mix, desk mixes, ambient mics, Click and LTC — follow the inputs.

And more.

⇧E makes the time selection from the selected clips; Export can export the selected clips and remembers your last choices; patching an output during playback no longer drops the audio; LTC picks the code up again after a pause.

//  What it is

A multitrack recorder for front of house, from any console through any audio interface. Built to be left running for a whole show and trusted, with the after-show work in the same window.

Every input, its own file.

Broadcast WAV or FLAC, 44.1 to 192 kHz: record at 48 kHz from a 96 kHz system, converted as it records. Every file is stamped with where it starts.

Built for the whole show.

Disk time against the show’s length before you start, a take recovered after a crash, every dropout counted and marked.

The mix, ready at Stop.

Render the mix during each take, WAV and MP3, through the Mixer and the mastering chain: hand it over that night.

Songs, mix and stems.

Find Songs splits the show at its gaps; export the stereo mix and the stems for the show, each song or a selection.

//  Your first session, in eight steps

Set it up before doors, at the desk or at home with the interface.

  1. Choose the interfaces

    Settings → Audio (⌘,): the Multitrack interface every track records from, and Sub-Mixer and Sync out for the mix, click and timecode.

  2. Start a session

    New Session (⌘N): empty, from last night’s session, from a template, or from the console.

  3. Bring in the console’s names

    Sync from Console (⌃⌥R) reads every channel’s name and colour from a dLive, an SQ, a Wing or a DiGiCo SD, through the MIDI Bridge 1.5 beta (a second download with this one), and shows what would change first.

  4. Patch

    Select tracks, type the first channel, then Inputs to cascade them, or click and drag in the Patch Matrix.

  5. Arm

    Click Arm at the top of the arm column, or ⌃⌥A. ⌥-click a track’s arm button to make it rec-safe.

  6. Check before you record

    The Recording panel: time left on the disk against the show’s length. Before You Record (⌃⌥P) lists anything that would make the take wrong.

  7. Record

    ⌃⌥Space, or 3 on the number pad. During a take Space only ever stops. At Stop the take is placed on its tracks and the session saved.

  8. Find the songs, export

    Find Songs (the Markers menu) makes a region for every song; Export (⌘E) writes the mix and the stems.

//  Built to be left running all night

What it does so you can watch the show, not the recorder.

A take is never lost.

If the app or the Mac stops mid-take, the take is recovered when the session next opens, up to the last moment written.

Dropouts, counted and marked.

The counter in the toolbar is zero in a quiet show. Each dropout is a ⚠ marker where it happened.

Every take, written twice.

Settings → Recording: a second drive gets the whole session. A slow or unplugged copy never touches the main recording.

The downbeat you were late for.

Pre-record: Off, 2, 5 or 10 s. Every take starts with the seconds before you pressed Record.

//  Timecode, built in

Patch the LTC row to the input carrying code, or follow MTC, and the header shows it: green when locked.

Every take at its code.

The first take that sees code sets the timeline to the hour; takes land at their code to the sample, so a run’s nights line up.

Record when timecode runs: AUTO.

The AUTO button in the transport row. A take starts when the code locks and stops when it’s been gone 5 seconds, or keeps recording until you stop it.

Playback follows the show.

Follow timecode in playback: a virtual soundcheck driven by the show’s own code. Never during a take.

LTC and MTC for the desk.

Play timecode on the LTC row’s output, or MTC out, so a console or show controller can chase Show Recorder.

//  Virtual soundcheck, through the desk

Every track plays back out its patched output: patch them to the console’s inputs and the show plays through the desk.

Out where it came in.

One click sets every track’s output to its input: the virtual soundcheck patch.

Soundcheck mute and solo.

Beside each output: what plays to the console. They change nothing recorded or exported.

The desk follows the show.

Scene recalls become markers during a take, named as the desk shows them; Recall scenes in playback puts the console in the right scene wherever you jump. On a dLive, a Wing or a DiGiCo SD, through MIDI Bridge.

Gapless, to the sample.

Drag across the ruler to loop a section; ⇧L switches it off and on. A take always runs straight through.

//  The mix and stems, before you leave

The Mixer is the export mix: it never touches the recording or the console.

A region for every song.

Find Songs splits at the L/R Mix’s quiet gaps, ready to rename. Run it again and the regions you placed are kept.

A mastering chain.

EQ, compressor, tape, clipper and limiter on the master; EQ and a compressor on each bus. Every stage heard live.

Mix and stems, per song.

WAV or MP3, normalized to a LUFS target under a true-peak ceiling if you like, with a CUE sheet for the whole show.

Loudness and phase.

The FOH meter: momentary, short-term and integrated loudness, true peak and correlation, of the L/R Mix as it comes in, or the master in playback.

//  Read the counter at the top of the window

Zero in a quiet show. Click it for the details, and Reset.

ZeroNothing lost: the disk, the interface and the engine all kept up.
A numberSomething cost the recording audio. Each is a ⚠ marker where it happened.
NOT RECORDINGThe take’s time stood still. Stop and start the take; if it happens again, a bigger buffer (Settings → Audio), and send the log.
A red inputPatched past the interface’s inputs: it records nothing. Record names it.
Load amber or redThe engine is near its deadline: a bigger buffer in Settings → Audio. It monitors nothing, so you hear no difference.

Something went wrong? Click Export log in the footer: a diagnostics zip lands on your Desktop. Send it with Send feedback beside it, or to dylan@dylanmaudio.com. Nothing leaves your Mac unless you send it.
