//  What it does

Someone talks on the shout bus, the surface flashes, you put the headphones on. Nothing to rig; one SoftKey to cancel. Talk Light Trigger watches a talkback mic or the shout bus and flashes the dLive's own surface lights while someone is talking — a talkback light, shout light or call light for the FOH engineer who can't hear the stage until the headphones go on, and for anyone who talks on cue.

//  Why I built it

I was mixing FOH without a shout box. The old one hadn’t been loud, but it had a signal light, and that was the useful part: when the light came on I knew someone on stage wanted me, and I’d get closer or put the headphones on. Then it broke mid-tour. The crew could talk all they liked; I couldn’t hear them until the headphones went on, and I had no way of knowing when that was — short of them texting me and hoping I’d look down. So I sent every talkback mic to a bus, and wrote something that watches that bus and flashes the console when there’s signal on it. The dLive has the lights already. It just needed telling when.

//  In detail

Watches the shout bus.

Any talkback mic, or a bus carrying all of them, on any input of any interface the Mac can see — Dante, SoundGrid, MADI, USB, Thunderbolt, PCIe.

Flashes the console’s own lights.

When signal crosses your threshold it recalls a “talk” Scene; when it stops, a “quiet” Scene. Each Scene carries a different Surface Illumination, and its Recall Filter is set to illumination only, so nothing else on the desk changes. Flash Rate alternates the two so the lights really flash; Flash Hold keeps them going for a few seconds after a pause.

Cancel from a SoftKey.

Assign a SoftKey to Custom MIDI — the panel shows you the exact string to type — and one press stops the flash the instant you’ve seen it. If they’re still talking after the cooldown, it comes back.

Doesn’t chatter.

Threshold, attack, release and a minimum retrigger time, tuned to the voice and the room, so breaths and pauses don’t set it off.

Tells you if the lights stop.

If Scenes stop reaching the console the panel turns red and says why. Through MIDI Bridge it reconnects by itself after a quiet spell. The session log records every Scene sent, every failure and every Cancel press, so an exported log shows what happened.

Gentle on the show interface.

One input channel, a large buffer, and it never changes the sample rate.

Connects the way you already do.

Direct TCP to the console with nothing else installed, or through the free MIDI Bridge. (The Cancel SoftKey needs a connection that can receive — Direct TCP, or a two-way MIDI port.)

Stream Deck control — and a Stream Deck that flashes too.

Run and Threshold on Bitfocus Companion keys with the talk state fed back, and a Talk flash page: when someone talks your Stream Deck jumps to a page of blinking keys, and goes back when they stop. With the free dylanmaudio Companion module.

//  Who uses it

FOH and monitor engineers who can’t hear the stage until the headphones go on. Anyone running a shout system, or who lost theirs. Podium, pit and booth setups where a mic going live should show on the desk. And as a plain call light: any signal on any input, and the console lights up.

//  Questions

Does it need a shout box?

No — that’s the point. It needs a talkback mic (or the bus they’re all on) reaching an input the Mac can see, and a dLive on the network.

Will it change anything else on the console?

Not if the two Scenes’ Recall Filters are set to Surface Illumination only, which is the setup the Quick Reference walks through. The Scenes then touch the lights and nothing else.

How do I stop it flashing once I’ve seen it?

A SoftKey. Set it to Custom MIDI with the string the panel shows and one press cancels the flash; it resumes after the cooldown if they’re still talking.

Can I use it as a cue light or a call light?

Yes. It doesn’t know what the signal is — any input crossing the threshold lights the desk.

What does it need?

A dLive; a Mac on the console’s network (Direct TCP, or through MIDI Bridge); the talkback signal on any audio input; and two Scenes on the console with different Surface Illumination.

Does it work with other consoles?

It’s built for the dLive, and users report it works on Allen & Heath Avantis as well — I haven’t tested that myself yet. Other consoles are being looked into — register your interest and say which desk.

Windows? Intel Macs?

Built for Apple Silicon. From this version the app also installs and runs on Intel Macs — verified so far in a VM, with testing on Intel hardware in October — so if you’re on Intel, try the free trial and tell me how it goes. Windows is being looked into — register your interest.

Does it phone home?

No analytics, no telemetry. Activating the licence is the only network call; Check for updates runs only when you press it. A licence covers two Macs — show machine and spare.
