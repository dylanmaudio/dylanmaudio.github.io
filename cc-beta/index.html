//  What it does

Every fader, mute, Scene, Action and send, programmed on a timeline against your show’s own audio and fired exactly when the show gets there. Drop in anywhere and the desk is right. The dLive is the first console; more manufacturers are on the way.

  • Fader, mute, Scene, Action and send cues on a timeline, locked to LTC or MTC
  • Import a live recording and every track lands at its real timecode — a whole set in under a minute
  • A tempo map from your click in seconds; snap cues to the beat
  • Your console's own Scene names, channel names and colours
  • Chase: locate anywhere and the console conforms
  • Preflight before doors, Show Mode during, Panic always
  • Works with MIDI Bridge, Time Code Tool and your Stream Deck

//  New in 0.2.1

Since 0.1.19, the build the first testers had. The full list comes with the download.

The keys are laid out afresh.

Single keys are cue programming and getting around; ⇧ is the pair of a single key; ⌥ is markers and regions; ⌘ is files, clipboard, undo and zoom; ⌃⌥ is the console and the view. If you used 0.1.19, many have moved — the keys below are the new ones, and every button shows its key when you hover over it.

Show Mode shows what’s coming next.

The region you’re in and how long is left of it, then the next cues, markers and regions, each with a countdown and its timecode. The countdown turns amber inside ten seconds and red inside three.

Sync from Console.

Reads every channel’s name and colour from the desk through MIDI Bridge, and shows what would change before anything does. New console channels are offered as tracks; nothing is added or deleted unless you say so.

A Mixer with EQ, compressor and limiter.

For the reference audio: an equaliser and an SSL-style bus compressor on every track, and a limiter on the master. Saved with the show and in every export.

Export Audio built for a show.

Master and timecode as separate files or one two-channel file, or every reference track; WAV up to 96 kHz and 32-bit float, or MP3; one region, every region, or the time selection. Timecode is generated at the export’s own sample rate.

Tempo detection follows the click.

A click that changes tempo gets a span for each steady stretch, and a ramp is followed beat by beat. Clicks are placed to a fraction of a frame.

Faster, sharper, more precise editing.

Select and move audio clips with a preview of where they land, zoom down to single frames, sharp waveforms on recordings hours long, and clip moves heard straight away instead of after a pause.

Runs on macOS 11 Big Sur or later.

Earlier betas needed macOS 13.3.

//  Your first session, in eight steps

Twenty minutes at the desk, or at home with a show recording. Nothing reaches the console until the bridge line is green.

  1. Start the dLive MIDI Bridge

    Console Control talks to the console through the free MIDI Bridge (1.1 or later). Wait for the green line at the bottom of the window.

  2. Match the base MIDI channel

    Settings (⌘,) — the same number as Utility → Control → MIDI on the console.

  3. Add your tracks

    + Track (⌘T), pick a type and a range. “1-16” makes sixteen input tracks in one step.

  4. Bring in the console’s names

    Sync from Console (⌃⌥R) for channel names and colours, straight from the desk. File → Import Console Show File for Scene names and Actions.

  5. Import a show recording

    ⌘I. With an LTC track present, every song is placed at its own timecode and outlined as a region.

  6. Place cues

    Select a track, then U, I or V for fader cues, M for mute, S for a Scene, A for an Action.

  7. Run Preflight

    ⌃⌥P checks the bridge, the base channel, chase and routing, and says exactly what is wrong.

  8. Arm SYNC, press play

    The timeline waits for timecode and follows it when it locks. Then ⌘⇧L for Show Mode.

//  Four ideas that explain everything else

If the app ever surprises you, it is almost always one of these.

Time, not bars — cues sit at a timecode position.

Live shows drift, drop the click and vamp. Tempo exists only for beat snapping and the grid — the ruler is always time.

Chase — move the playhead and the desk follows.

Scrub, locate or play: every lane’s value at that position is worked out and sent. Chase can be switched off for everything (⇧C) or per lane (C).

Fired vs pending — plain text was sent, a dot was not.

A dot beside a readout means “the console should be here, but it hasn’t been sent”. Conform Console sends everything pending in one pass.

One console path — everything goes through MIDI Bridge.

Console Control never opens its own connection to the desk, so your DAW, Companion and the other apps keep working beside it.

//  SYNC arms, play latches, stop disarms

The button beside Record. Always this way round — a show never latches on its own when it opens.

DimmedSync off. Internal clock, for programming and loop playback.
Lit steel-blueArmed. Press play to latch.
Pulsing amberPlay pressed, awaiting timecode.
GreenLocked to incoming timecode. Locate and scrub belong to the source.
Steady amberFreewheeling — the signal dropped and the timeline is coasting.

Right-click SYNC for the source (LTC or MTC), the LTC input channel, the MTC port, freewheel time, drift tolerance and offset. Still pulsing while code plays? It is listening to the wrong input.

//  Built for show day

Four controls to know before doors.

Preflight — ⌃⌥P

Flags problems before the show: bridge and console link, base-channel mismatches, chase switched off, send lanes the console can’t route.

Show Mode — ⌘⇧L

Locks editing and undo. Huge timecode, sync, chase, bridge health and live per-track readouts — and what’s coming next, with countdowns. Asks before it enters and before it leaves.

Conform Console — ⌃⌥C

A one-shot chase you can use at any moment. Play always conforms first, so the console enters playback in the right state.

Panic — ⌘⇧Esc

Always available, even with everything else locked.

//  On your Stream Deck

Beta testers get Stream Deck control too, through Bitfocus Companion.

Every shortcut is a key.

Through the free Companion module, Console Control’s commands are Companion actions — play, Conform, Show Mode, Panic, cue programming — with sync lock and chase shown on the keys. A Stream Deck is the nicest way to press them; Companion’s web buttons on a phone or tablet work too.

Set it up once.

Install Companion on the same Mac and add the dylanmaudio module — the steps are on the Companion page. Console Control appears beside your other dylanmaudio apps.

You decide what it can touch.

Settings → Companion: allow Companion control or switch it off, and lock the show-critical controls — Stop, Record, Conform and Show Mode are refused while locked. Panic always works.

//  If something is wrong

Read the line at the bottom of the window. It tells you where the chain is broken, in order.

GreenConsole connected through the bridge. Its IP is shown.
AmberBridge running, console unreachable. Check the console IP in the bridge, the network, and MIDI-over-TCP on the console.
RedThe dLive MIDI Bridge app isn’t running. Start it.
Green, but nothing movesCheck chase for everything (⇧C), the track’s M, the lane’s chase badge — or run Preflight (⌃⌥P).
A track won’t editIt is locked, or you are in Show Mode.

Something went wrong? Click Export log in the footer — a copy of the session log lands on your Desktop. Send it with Send feedback beside it, or to dylan@dylanmaudio.com. The log already records your settings, the show, every message you saw and the timecode state.
